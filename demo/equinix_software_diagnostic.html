01What the software business isBusiness

Equinix is a REIT that owns 282 data centers, 179 of them outright. The software exists to make that footprint programmable and to keep customers from needing to leave it.

  • Fabric. Virtual interconnection. Buy a port, spin circuits to clouds, networks, and SaaS in minutes.
  • Fabric Cloud Router. Virtual routing instance. Reach several destinations at Layer 3 with no hardware in the cage.
  • Fabric Geo Zones. Network level geofencing for data residency, announced Q2 2026, in preview with 80 enterprises.
  • Network Edge. Virtual network functions from Palo Alto, Fortinet, Cisco, deployed in minutes instead of shipped to a cage.
  • Fabric Intelligence. Launched April 2026. Super Agent, MCP server, Application Connect marketplace, Fabric Insights telemetry.
  • Distributed AI Hub. Launched March 2026. Deploys AI workloads across geographies without the customer managing the networking underneath.
  • Metal. Sunset June 30, 2026. The one major software bet the company has killed, and the most useful case in the portfolio. Analyzed in section 02.

02How the business got here, 2001 to 2026New

Twenty five years in five year blocks. Each block asks one question: where did the money come from, and what did that force the company to become. The arc runs from a near bankruptcy rescue, through interconnection becoming the margin engine, through the REIT conversion and the cloud arriving as customer and threat at once, through a genuine run at platform ambition that Metal's failure eventually capped, and back to software again in 2026, this time built strictly around the physical asset rather than around compute. The point of the exercise is the pattern that falls out at the end, laid out in the table below: every product that stayed tied to the facility survived, and the one that did not was killed. That pattern is the strongest available evidence for what the software line can and cannot become.

2001 to 2005
Survival, and the accidental discovery of the real product

Equinix went public in August 2000 and raised more than $270M months before the sector collapsed. That cash bought runway and it was not enough. By 2002 the company was one payroll from extinction and the operator community was openly speculating about a bankruptcy filing.

The rescue came in October 2002: a strategic investment from Singapore Technologies Telemedia, a merger of i-STT and Pihana Pacific into the exchange business, retirement of roughly 80% of outstanding bonds, and $30M of new cash. Going in, the pitch was scale. Coming out, management refocused on interconnection and ecosystem services rather than raw square footage. Square footage is a commodity anyone with capital can build. Interconnection density is a network effect that compounds and cannot be bought.

2006 to 2010
Interconnection becomes the margin engine

The strategy narrowed to metro clusters engineered for density. Get the tier one backbones, the content networks, and the CDNs into the same handful of buildings, and every new tenant makes the building more valuable to the next one. The 2010 acquisition of Switch and Data for roughly $689M extended the model into second tier US markets rather than diluting it.

Space and power paid the rent. Cross connects carried the margin. This is the first period where a non real estate line materially drove the P&L, and it sets the template: a service sold on top of the facility, only purchasable because you are already in the facility.

2011 to 2015
Financialization, and the cloud arrives as customer and threat at once

The board approved a plan to pursue REIT conversion in September 2012, formally approved it in December 2014, and Equinix began operating as a REIT on January 1, 2015. In May 2015 the IRS issued a favorable private letter ruling that treated data center assets as qualified REIT assets and, critically, interconnection revenues as qualified REIT revenues.

In parallel the strategic problem of the era took shape. Hyperscalers were becoming the largest tenants and the largest long term threat simultaneously. The answer, launched in 2014, was Cloud Exchange: software defined connectivity to AWS, Azure, and Google from a single port. That product is the direct ancestor of Fabric. The period closed with Telecity announced in May 2015 at roughly £2.4B, alongside Bit-isle in Japan at $280M.

2016 to 2020
Peak platform ambition

Telecity closed in January 2016, taking the portfolio from 112 data centers in 33 metros to 145 in 40. Cloud Exchange became Equinix Fabric. Network Edge added virtual network functions. In July 2019 the first xScale joint venture with GIC committed more than $1B to hyperscale facilities, a structural admission that hyperscalers needed a different product than the retail IBX.

Then the boldest move. In March 2020 Equinix acquired Packet for roughly $335M and by October launched Equinix Metal: dedicated servers provisioned in minutes, integrated with Fabric, sold through APIs and developer tooling.

2021 to 2025
Retrenchment on software, escalation on land and power

Metal never reached scale. By the time the decision was made it accounted for well under 2% of revenue while competing against HPE, IBM, Oracle, the hyperscalers, and dedicated bare metal providers simultaneously. Commercial sales stopped and the sunset was announced, with leadership stating the focus was returning to colocation, interconnection, and hyperscale.

The capital story went the other direction. In October 2024 Equinix signed a joint venture with GIC and CPP Investments intending to raise more than $15B, nearly tripling the xScale program, targeting campuses above 100MW and more than 1.5GW of new capacity. Adaire Fox-Martin took over as CEO. By Q4 2025 annualized gross bookings hit $1.6B, up 27%, with roughly 60% of the largest deals driven by AI workloads.

2026 to present
Software returns, redefined around the asset

Metal was formally retired on June 30, 2026. In its place a different shape of software shipped: Distributed AI Hub in March, Fabric Intelligence in April, and Fabric Geo Zones announced in Q2. None of them sells compute. All of them sell something that only works because of where the buildings are.

The pattern, and why it is the most useful thing on this page

Metal is the control case. It was well funded, well timed on paper, launched by credible operators, and integrated with Fabric. It failed anyway, because a developer choosing bare metal had no structural reason to choose Equinix over a dozen alternatives. That is the structural test in section 06, demonstrated rather than asserted, with twenty five years of company behavior behind it.

The structural constraint almost nobody outside finance is holding. The 2015 IRS ruling qualified interconnection revenue as REIT revenue because it is tied to the property. Revenue that is not tied to the property does not get that treatment and sits in a taxable REIT subsidiary. The corporate structure itself rewards software attached to the facility and penalizes software that stands alone. Any conversation about how large the software line can become runs into this before it runs into product quality or market size.

Caveat. Figures are from filings, press releases, and contemporaneous reporting, reproduced as reported. The 2002 near failure detail comes from executive recollection rather than a filing, so treat the specific characterization loosely and the outcome as documented.

04How the open weight wave drives demandThesis

The CEO named four AI demand patterns on the Q2 call: private AI stacks to cut token costs, sovereign deployments for residency, AI factory batch inference often requiring liquid cooling, and latency sensitive metro inference. Three of the four are the open weight transition described in enterprise language.

The mechanism

  • Self hosting became rational. Once a capable open weight model sits within a couple of points of the frontier, the enterprise question stops being which model and becomes where it runs and who controls the data path.
  • Cost per task, not per token. Artificial Analysis measures average cost to complete its Intelligence Index rather than sticker price, because a model that looks cheap per token can still cost more when it takes extra steps. DeepSeek V4 Flash came in near $0.03 per test against $3.15 for Claude Fable 5, with GPT 5.6 Sol at $1.86 and Kimi K3 at $0.86.
  • Agentic traffic is bursty and machine to machine. Dynamic bandwidth between fixed endpoints is the shape of a Fabric connection.
  • Inference sprawls where training concentrates. Training sits in a few campuses. Inference follows users and data, which re rates a distributed footprint.
McKinsey, cited in Equinix AI Summit keynote

Equinix cites a McKinsey forecast showing AI inference's share of data center capacity rising from roughly 30% in 2025 to roughly 70% by 2029, with training's share falling by the same amount. An independent forecast, not an Equinix internal number, and it measures share of capacity rather than absolute volume, so it supports the inference sprawl mechanism above without resolving whether that inference lands inside third party interconnected space or captive hyperscaler infrastructure.

Three tensions to carry, not paper over. The pricing above is metered API pricing on someone else's hardware, so on its own it argues for renting open weight inference rather than self hosting it. Cheap inference is also deflationary per workload, so the win is many more inference endpoints in many more places rather than higher spend per customer. And the cost gap sits between models at different capability tiers, since V4 Flash scores 50 on the Intelligence Index. That shows the cheap tier becoming good enough for routine work rather than the frontier commoditizing.

Evidence running the other way, added August 10. US hyperscaler cloud revenue growth moved from roughly 11% in early 2025 to 43% by Q3 2026 on $364B of trailing revenue, with segment operating margins holding or improving through the buildout. That is consistent with AI demand concentrating in captive infrastructure rather than fragmenting across third party operators. The counterargument, that heavier hyperscaler consumption means more multi cloud estates needing private paths between them, is plausible and not demonstrated by this data.

05Where the software sits in the AI stackThesis

Eight layers, mapped to a construction site. The model is the carpenter. The harness is the job site, which splits into context and memory. Orchestration is the build sequence. Inference is materials consumed. Latency is hours on the job. Below those sit the machines and the lot.

Coverage marks what Equinix as a company owns. The software column is narrower on purpose. Two of eight layers carry an Equinix software surface. The bottom two rows are facilities rather than product.

The software is the hookup and the lifecycle, not the job site

Fabric, Cloud Router, Network Edge, the Terraform provider, and the MCP server provision and control private paths. They do not assemble context, hold memory, or run an agent loop. That work lives in the customer's codebase, and open source and Microsoft are both driving its price toward zero. Equinix should sell to the people building agent platforms rather than build one.

Model independence is now stated policy at both ends of the market

Microsoft says its evals should keep improving even when any given model is removed, and keeps the surrounding memory, context, tools, and skills outside the model on purpose. Andrew Ng's OpenWorker ships with no inference service at all and routes to whichever provider the user supplies. DeepSeek prices routine work at a fraction of frontier cost. Customers running several models across several providers need deterministic private paths between them.

Satya Nadella, August 2026. OpenWorker launch, July 23, 2026. Artificial Analysis, reported August 2026.
Yang Song, Equinix Chief Data Science and AI Officer · Aug 27, 2026

Equinix's own AI officer makes this same argument in its own words: falling per token model prices raise, not lower, total AI bills, citing an outside estimate that the model invoice is only 28% of total production AI cost, with the rest in orchestration, retrieval, retries, and observability. The stated fix is intelligent routing across models, explicitly tied to Fabric Intelligence and Distributed AI Hub. This upgrades the evidence from other companies behaving as if model independence matters to Equinix stating outright that it is the reason to buy its connectivity, though it is Equinix marketing its own products, not independent data on adoption.

Latency is structural, and it is the meter Equinix owns

Serving inference at scale relies on batching, which buys throughput by adding wait. Generating each token also requires streaming the model weights and the growing context out of memory, so long context and multi step agent loops slow down as they run. A better model fixes neither. Token cost is a vendor negotiation. Milliseconds are distance.

Custom inference hardware pools memory across chips so a rack behaves like one machine. Agent workloads chain a model call, a data lookup, and several tool calls, each crossing a network that this work leaves alone. That gap is where the software division lives.

Etched investment memo, August 2026, written by the investor, so the vendor specifics are held loosely.
Equinix AI Summit session, The Infrastructure Mismatch

Equinix puts numbers on this directly: AI racks draw 40 to 120 plus kilowatts against 5 to 10 for a traditional rack, and real time inference needs under 10 milliseconds of latency against 100 to 500 milliseconds tolerated by traditional workloads. The same slide frames single cloud provider architecture against multicloud and specialized AI providers as a partner risk, the same neutrality argument Yang Song makes above, and pairs security as multi environment governance and data sovereignty against a traditional perimeter model.

06Where the software can goThesis

With a monetization path

  • A metered control plane. Fabric Intelligence is a feature today; per seat or per action pricing makes it a software line on top of circuit revenue.
  • Marketplace economics on Fabric Application Connect, with 2,000 plus AI ecosystem partners already on the supply side.
  • Cost attribution for AI traffic, extending Fabric Insights into the FinOps conversation where AI budget authority sits.
  • Security service insertion. Prisma AIRS on Network Edge is the template for every vendor that needs to sit in the path of agent traffic.
  • Sovereign inference bundles: compute, interconnection, jurisdiction, and policy sold as one contract. Geo Zones is the first piece of this shipping.

Without one, correctly

  • The Terraform provider and published modules. Adoption infrastructure, so an engineer can standardize without a procurement conversation.
  • Playground and the developer platform. A funnel rather than a product.
  • Documentation and OpenAPI quality. Every hour of friction is a circuit bought somewhere else.
Equinix AI Summit, Interconnection Evolution

Equinix states its global network covers 70 plus metros with a 35 percent plus share of cloud on ramps, and describes its ecosystem layer as roughly 3,000 cloud and IT service providers plus 2,000 plus network service providers, a broader figure than the 2,000 plus AI ecosystem partners cited above for Application Connect specifically. Worth keeping as two separate counts rather than merging the general partner base with the AI marketplace figure.

The structural test. A control layer makes the layer beneath it substitutable. Terraform did exactly that to clouds. Software that is only valuable because of where it runs does the opposite. Application Connect is the right shape, because a private path to inference capacity is worth more from a building that contains the capacity. A generic control plane for distributed AI infrastructure is the wrong shape, because it works equally well pointed at someone else's footprint. If the software would work just as well against a competitor's facility, it makes Equinix more replaceable.

Section 02 is the empirical case for this. Every product that passed the test survived. The one that failed it was killed.

SAP parallel

The same tension shows up as ERP versus platform and data services. Both are real products, but only one is tied to what the company structurally owns and controls end to end. Equinix owns the buildings the way SAP owned the core transactional system, and the products that survive here are the ones that make that ownership harder to route around, not the ones that could run just as well somewhere else.

Equinix AI Summit, Global Network target state

Equinix's own stated AI ready target is 400 plus Gbps per customer connection and 5 million plus interconnections, against a current 492,000 plus, alongside a shift from manual log review and portal orders to dynamic AI optimizations. Treat this as an aspirational target the company is stating publicly, not a disclosed current metric, but it is a clean articulation of what the cost attribution and observability monetization path above is actually meant to replace.

Added August 10. Expansion should go up in value rather than up the stack. The legitimate moves all depend on owning facilities: latency sold as a measured commitment rather than best effort connectivity, placement guidance based on where a customer's models and data actually sit, complete lifecycle control surfaces, and data gravity services. Everything above the metal is being commoditized in public by companies with more money and more reason to do it.

07The next four quarters: paths, bets, and how it breaksNew

Projections rather than forecasts. Everything here is judgment built on public disclosure through Q2 2026 and the pattern in section 02. Read the tells rather than the predictions. The tells are checkable in real time; the predictions are not.

Written from three seats deliberately, because they disagree in useful places. On the Q2 call the CEO framed elevating the value proposition through the Fabric offerings as a specific mandate belonging to the new chief product officer, so the division now has a named owner, a public commitment, and a clock.

The board seat

Why does a sub 2% revenue line justify a chief product officer, and what stops this becoming Metal again. Instinct is to cap ambition and treat headcount intensive motions with suspicion because of what they do to the multiple.

The analyst seat

Which line moves, by how much, and can it be seen from outside. Notices the software story has growth rates but no levels, and that the one disclosed growth rate could as easily be substitution as expansion.

The CEO seat

Does this person have enough real ownership to stay, and enough constraint to not repeat history. Cares about org design over roadmap, because a product leader without a number becomes a feature factory.

Where the three seats collide

Board versus analyst. The board wants proof software defends the interconnection franchise. The analyst points out that proof does not exist publicly and probably does not exist internally in clean form either. Neither can settle it, which produces the first conclusion: the opening deliverable is not a product, it is instrumentation.

Analyst versus CEO. A virtual router replacing three physical cross connects looks like software growth while quietly compressing interconnection revenue per customer. The CEO argues that is acceptable if switching cost and total wallet rise. Both are right. The resolution is that the metric has to be revenue and stickiness per customer per metro, not revenue per connection type. The exposure is that if an analyst asks this on a call before there is an answer, it is a bad quarter.

Board versus CEO. The board wants scope narrowed because of Metal. The CEO knows a narrowed leader leaves inside eighteen months. The resolution is asymmetric: narrow the product surface, widen the organizational mandate. Give the division every customer facing automation surface in the company, including ones attached to products it does not own, and very few new products to launch.

How it goes sideways, ordered by likelihood

Death by preview

Three products sit in preview against one organization. None reaches general availability with real pricing because each is nearly ready and none is finished. Twelve months pass, preview counts grow, revenue does not, and patience runs out. This is the highest probability failure and it is entirely internal. It requires no competitor, no market shift, and no technical problem. The counterintuitive fix is to defer one of the three immediately, because an org holding three previews is signaling it has not chosen.

Too fast on automation, and a trust event

Agentic provisioning ships broadly before the lifecycle is complete. An agent creates infrastructure it cannot remove, the orphaned resource keeps carrying production traffic, and the incident is customer visible. Enterprise trust in programmatic control collapses and buyers retreat to manual provisioning, which sets the strategy back further than never shipping it. This is not hypothetical: the Fabric MCP server's missing delete on routers, ports, and connections is exactly the precondition, and it is the cheapest item here to fix.

Quiet substitution

Virtual routing grows fast while physically replacing higher margin connections. The visible signal is software success. The invisible signal is interconnection revenue per customer flattening while connection counts rise. Because nobody tracks it in that shape it goes unnoticed for several quarters, then arrives as an unexplained margin question on a call. Watch for divergence between interconnection adds and interconnection revenue growth.

Drift to attach, and departure

Nothing dramatic fails. Software never gets its own number, gets folded into the interconnection story, and the division dissolves into an operations function. The product leader leaves within eighteen to twenty four months and the cycle repeats. This is the Metal pattern without the writeoff, and the corporate structure quietly favors it.

Demand shape change rather than demand collapse

The threat is not that AI demand falls, it is that it lands somewhere else. Three variants: concentration, where inference consolidates into captive hyperscaler and neocloud facilities and the neutrality premium erodes exactly when it looked strongest; efficiency, where a step change in model efficiency reduces the volume of distributed infrastructure required; and vertical integration, where a hyperscaler builds a genuinely neutral fabric across its own and partner facilities, attacking the franchise with software rather than buildings. None shows up inside four quarters. The third is the only scenario where software, not real estate, loses the company its position.

Structural ceiling

Standalone software and services revenue is non qualifying income in a taxable subsidiary. At current scale there is room. If the line ever grew large, the structure itself becomes the constraint and the choice is between capping ambition or reconsidering trust status. Nobody faces this within four quarters. It is the reason the ambition has a ceiling unrelated to product quality. See section 02.

Services, and why it is the strongest idea here

The first of the four named AI demand patterns is enterprises running open models on private infrastructure to cut token cost. That customer has a real problem Equinix does not currently solve. They need model selection, a serving stack, a harness, routing, evaluation infrastructure, hardware procurement, and network design. Equinix supplies space, power, connectivity, and now residency. The gap between what is sold and what the customer needs to succeed is people, and that gap is filled today by nobody or by an integrator with no stake in the facility.

Why this is not Metal. This is the objection that will be raised and it deserves a direct answer. Metal sold compute, a commodity available from a dozen vendors with no structural reason to buy it from a colocation provider. A forward deployed motion sells nothing of the kind. It sells the ability to make the customer's own compute work inside Equinix facilities, using Equinix connectivity, under Equinix sovereignty controls. Inseparable from the asset, which is the test in section 06. There is also precedent the board will recognize: the company bought a professional services firm in 2015 to help customers transition into hybrid architectures. The motion is not foreign. It has simply never been treated as strategic.

Equinix AI Summit, AI Solutions Lab

Equinix already runs an AI Solutions Lab across 20 Solution Validation Center locations in 10 countries, explicitly chartered to collaborate with AI providers, validate next generation AI solutions, and prove out customer blueprints, close to the reusable reference architecture motion argued for below. Delivery is explicitly partner led, with Deloitte and Accenture named as AI consulting providers alongside Verizon, AMD, Intel, and Dell, which is evidence the capped, partner delivered shape already exists in some form rather than being a bet still to be made. It does not settle volume, whether any engagement has produced a named reference, or whether the program is funded the way the shape below recommends.

SAP parallel

SAP tried staffing implementation and customization internally for years, and it was consistently outcompeted by its own partner network, since partners could adapt the software across industry and geography far faster than an internal org built for one central roadmap ever could. The capped, twenty person version proposed below only works if it stays a requirements pipeline rather than trying to become the delivery org. The moment headcount grows past that, it repeats the exact motion SAP eventually had to hand back to partners.

Board on services

Hostile on arrival. Headcount intensive, low margin, non qualifying income, dilutes the multiple the equity story rests on. Receptive only if capped, funded as customer acquisition expense, and measured on pull through rather than billings.

Analyst on services

Indifferent to services revenue, too small to model. Cares about one ratio: recurring bookings generated per dollar of deployed engineering cost. Above roughly five to one it is the cheapest acquisition channel in the company. Below three to one it is consulting in a strategy costume.

CEO on services

Most enthusiastic, for an organizational reason. You cannot hire two hundred platform engineers into a colocation company and hold the culture. You can hire twenty who sit with customers, and they become the requirements pipeline that stops the product org from guessing.

The shape that survives all three seats. Capped headcount in the low tens. Funded as sales and marketing expense, never booked as a services revenue line. Deployed only against the private AI stack pattern, not spread across every customer request. Chartered to convert every engagement into a reusable reference architecture, with a hard rule that the same problem is never solved bespoke twice. Measured on recurring bookings pulled through and product requirements generated, never on utilization or services margin. Reviewed at four quarters against the ratio, with genuine willingness to shut it down.

Every other bet on this page improves something that exists. This one changes what the company can learn. The central weakness of the software line across its whole history is that it builds for customers it observes through sales reports rather than through engineers in the room. Metal was designed for a developer nobody at Equinix had watched work.

Equinix, Distributed AI in action

Equinix's own customer facing message for this motion is prove ROI before investing in your own GPU infrastructure, marketing language for the same build versus buy decision argued above. Thin on its own, but it confirms some version of this motion is already being sold to customers rather than purely proposed internally, alongside language on retrieval augmented generation that is consistent with section 05's coverage table, Equinix positioned as the plumbing under a RAG deployment rather than owning RAG itself.

08Which sales motion the software line requiresThesis

Two questions decide an enterprise go to market motion. How exposed is the person who signs, and does social proof travel in that market.

A network engineer provisioning a connection carries low personal exposure. The work is additive, reversible, not a system of record, and not customer facing. Reputation there travels engineer to engineer through documentation and hands on trials rather than by marquee logo. Both conditions point the same way: win on math and speed. Time to first working connection, cost against alternatives, and whether an evaluator can get moving alone.

That makes the free tier, the documentation, and the automation surfaces the go to market rather than a convenience. It also puts the software line at odds with the core facilities motion, which is long cycle, high exposure, and relationship driven. A company built end to end for the second motion will feel friction running the first, in pricing, in sales compensation, in how trials work, and in who owns the developer experience.

SAP parallel

A sales organization built and compensated for long cycle enterprise relationships will always underinvest in a self serve motion, since the incentives point the same salesperson toward the bigger, slower deal every time. This was true at SAP with a data and platform motion competing for attention against ERP, and it shows up here the same way, in trial design, in pricing, and in who actually owns the developer experience.

Read this way, the missing delete is a specific kind of defect. It surfaces in the first hour of an evaluation and loses the deal quietly, with nobody filing a complaint.

None of this is a demand generation motion

Every use case above assumes a customer who already has an Equinix relationship, physical or virtual. Nobody searches for Fabric cold. Discovery runs through an account team that already understands the customer's environment, which makes the growth engine internal account expansion rather than inbound interest. This is the same low exposure, win on math and speed dynamic named in section 08, and the same land and expand mechanic described in section 02's REIT structure discussion. Land and expand is a real, defensible advantage once a customer is in, and it is a structural limit on how the software line grows a customer base that was not there to begin with.

The Megaport comparison

Section 10 already flags Megaport as tenant and competitor at once: it reaches over a thousand facilities by riding other operators' floor space, including Equinix's own, rather than owning buildings. That is worth stating plainly rather than as a caveat: the lack of a land strategy is not a handicap in this comparison, it is the mechanism. Megaport shows up wherever a customer already sits, unconstrained by Equinix's own physical footprint.

Public review sentiment points the same direction: Megaport rated 4.8 against Fabric's 4.3 on G2, with onboarding speed named specifically and repeatedly as the differentiator, not price and not the underlying connectivity claims.

Hold this as a hypothesis, not a proven cause. The G2 sample is thin, 11 Fabric reviews against 166 for Megaport, so this is directional signal, not a settled finding. What would settle it is real internal win loss data, actual deals lost to Megaport with documented reasons, which almost certainly exists inside Equinix already and is not visible from outside. Worth validating against that the moment it is accessible, rather than carrying outside review sentiment into a room as proof.

The vertical motion is real, and it is a genuine structural advantage. Equinix runs dedicated financial services and healthcare solution pages, cites 1,300+ financial services customers, and sells a named security product, SmartKey, specifically into that vertical. The facility level certifications, HIPAA, PCI DSS, FISMA, are earned once at the building and available to every customer in that vertical without separate audit, which a facility light competitor cannot claim the same way. This is the one piece of the comparison where owning the land is unambiguously the advantage rather than the constraint.
