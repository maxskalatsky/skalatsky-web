    How the flags are computed

    Scoring weights — judgment calls, stated openly
    Every flag carries an anomaly score (0–100) built from weighted factors, the same way the TCPED copilot shows its scoring work. The weights are judgment calls, not physics:

    Dark: gap hours × 6 (cap 40) + off-lane (0–20) + rendezvous geometry (0–25) + type prior (0–15). A long silence in open water scores; a short gap near port does not.
    Spoof: implied-speed component (cap 60) + MMSI reuse (25) + lane mismatch (0–15). Impossible physics is the strongest single signal in the set.
    Loiter: loiter hours × 4 (cap 40) + distance from fishing grounds (0–30) + slow speed (0–15). Circles are cheap; circles far from fish are expensive.

    Each assessment also states its confidence and what would change it — a flag without a falsifier is just an accusation. All thresholds are synthetic and illustrative.
    How the triage loop works
    The queue ranks every flagged contact by anomaly score. Accept tasks a contact for investigation — it gets a white ring on the map and moves to the accepted list. Dismiss requires a reason (false positive, equipment fault, already tasked, insufficient evidence), because a dismissal without a reason is un-auditable. Anything can be undone, and the triage log is the audit trail. The loop — not the detection — is the product: models flag, humans decide, and every decision is recorded.
    How this maps to TCPED
    This prototype is the front half of the TCPED cycle that the triage copilot compresses:

    Tasking — an AIS cue becomes a contact worth watching.
    Collection — the contact's track history, gap geometry, and MMSI record.
    Processing — anomaly scoring, with the work shown.
    Exploitation — the analyst's accept/dismiss loop on this page.
    Dissemination — an accepted contact leaves here as a track summary for the watch floor — exactly the kind of tasking the TCPED copilot queues, scores, and routes to the right analyst.

    That last step is live: accepting a contact unlocks Disseminate → TCPED, which hands the contact to the copilot as a URL-encoded tasking. Priority maps from the anomaly score (85+ → 5/5), confidence from the assessment, and the detection narrative rides along into the copilot's decision modal. The card carries a ◈ tag so the architecture stays visible: detection → triage → tasking.

    Same visual family, same design bets: explainable scores, stated weights, synthetic data labeled throughout, and the human decision as the product.
