    How this works

    The explainer boxes, collapsed so they don't take fixed space. Everything on this page is synthetic.

    Synthetic data
    Every tasking on this page is generated in your browser by a small script — no real vessels, no real sources, no HII or SOUTHCOM claims. The point is the shape of the workflow: taskings arrive, they wait, somebody has to triage them.
    How the copilot scores
    Every tasking gets three numbers: mission priority (what kind of contact it is), source confidence (how reliable the cueing source is), and team load — measured, not guessed: how busy the analysts are and how deep the queue runs. Score = 0.5 × priority + 0.3 × confidence + 0.2 × load, scaled 0–100. Each card shows its work — a triage copilot that can't explain itself won't be trusted.
    Capacity is the constraint
    Four analysts, one tasking each at a time. Higher-priority taskings take more cycles — they get worked more thoroughly. A specialty match (imagery analyst on a port-activity check) shortens the job. Idle analysts always claim the highest-scored tasking waiting. When the queue climbs while all four bars are full, you're looking at the absorption problem. Time is compressed: one 4-second tick ≈ 10 watch-minutes, so a 7-cycle tasking stands in for roughly 70 minutes of real work. The durations are illustrative; the queueing dynamics are the point.
    Shadow deployment
    A second, headless watch floor runs the same taskings — identical priority, confidence, and arrival times — under the old rule: every idle analyst grabs the highest-scored tasking, no specialty consideration. The visible floor uses copilot routing. The difference is measured, not asserted.
    How the copilot routes
    For each idle analyst and each waiting tasking, fit = score + 15 for a specialty match. Assign the best pair, remove both, repeat. The 15-point bonus is a judgment call, stated openly — it's the value the copilot places on putting the right analyst on the right tasking.
    Dark-vessel handoffs
    Accepted contacts from the dark-vessel triage page can be disseminated here as taskings (?inject=[...]). They arrive pre-triaged — scored by the detection page — so they skip this floor's dedupe merge and land as their own cards, twinned on both floors to keep the comparison fair. The ◈ tag marks the architecture: detection → triage → tasking. Priority maps from the anomaly score (85+ → 5/5); confidence from the assessment (High → 88%).

    What this demonstrates

    The year-one lever, made concrete.

    1
    Absorption, not volume. Arrivals outpace four analysts. That gap — not a shortage of collection — is the actual bottleneck.
    2
    Same capacity, better allocation. The copilot adds no analysts. It changes what they see first, and the shadow floor measures the difference.
    3
    Routing is the product. Ranking is a commodity. Putting the right analyst on the right tasking is where the time comes from.
